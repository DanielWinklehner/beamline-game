    What's real in this game

    Every dot is one of 2,000 simulated protons, each with its own small offset and angle. The game tracks all of them through your line and counts how many survive.

    • Quadrupoles are thin lenses. Each focuses in one plane and defocuses in the other, which is why the top and side views differ. Alternating them gives net focusing in both planes, the principle behind every large accelerator since the 1950s.
    • Magnet strength is fixed, so a magnet's focal length grows with the protons' momentum. Faster beams are stiffer and harder to bend.
    • Each RF cavity adds 5 MeV. Angles shrink in proportion to the momentum gain (adiabatic damping), so the beam tightens as it accelerates.
    • The pipe wall is a hard aperture. A proton that touches it is lost, and the glow on the wall shows where losses pile up.
    • The bending magnet is a 90° sector dipole. The field it needs is set by the beam's momentum (its magnetic rigidity, Bρ = p/e), shown in real tesla for a 1.27 m bending radius. A sector dipole also focuses gently in the bend plane.
    • The game forgives field errors far more than a real machine would. Real dipoles are matched to the beam within a few parts in ten thousand.
    • In the workshop, beam current adds linear space charge, which weakens as the beam speeds up (it scales with 1/(βγ)³).
    • Left out for now: RF timing and bunching, energy spread (and the dispersion it causes in a bend), and nonlinear fields.
