    What's real in this game

    Every dot is one of 2,000 simulated protons, each with its own small offset and angle. The game tracks all of them through your line and counts how many survive.

    • Quadrupoles are thin lenses. Each focuses in one plane and defocuses in the other, which is why the top and side views differ. Alternating them gives net focusing in both planes, the principle behind every large accelerator since the 1950s.
    • Magnet strength is fixed, so a magnet's focal length grows with the protons' momentum. Faster beams are stiffer and harder to bend.
    • Each RF cavity adds 5 MeV. Angles shrink in proportion to the momentum gain (adiabatic damping), so the beam tightens as it accelerates.
    • The pipe wall is a hard aperture. A proton that touches it is lost, and the glow on the wall shows where losses pile up.
    • The bending magnet is a 90° sector dipole. The field it needs is set by the beam's momentum (its magnetic rigidity, Bρ = p/e), shown in real tesla for a 1.27 m bending radius. A sector dipole also focuses gently in the bend plane.
    • The game forgives field errors far more than a real machine would. Real dipoles are matched to the beam within a few parts in ten thousand.
    • In the workshop, beam current adds linear space charge, which weakens as the beam speeds up (it scales with 1/(βγ)³).
    • In the cyclotron, 360 protons are tracked lap by lap. Each lap they cross two gaps and gain up to 1 MeV, times the cosine of their phase. That is a generous push for a real machine, chosen so the spiral stays readable.
    • A proton laps at f = qB/(2πγm). The frequency only stays constant if the average field rises in step with γ, so the shims have to lift the field toward the edge (an isochronous cyclotron). Any mismatch makes the phase slip a little every lap. Every bunch gets its push at the same moment, so in a well-shaped field the bunches line up like the hand of a clock. When the timing slips, that line curls into a spiral arm. The animation shows only a few bunches so you can follow them, where a real cyclotron carries one in every RF period.
    • A field that rises with radius pushes protons out of the midplane. Hills and valleys add flutter, and the vertical tune follows Thomas's result, νz² ≈ −k + F², for straight sectors. The flat pole falls off slightly toward the edge, which gives weak focusing until you shim against it.
    • The cyclotron uses the orbit-averaged field. The central region, radial betatron motion, resonances and the extraction system are left out, and the scalloped orbits are drawn for looks.
    • Left out for now: RF bunching in the linac, energy spread (and the dispersion it causes in a bend), and nonlinear fields.
